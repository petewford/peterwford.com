ASOS · Refer a Friend

A referral programme to boost customer acquisition in the US

Role
Product designer
Timeline
Jan – Oct 2019
Disciplines
UX, UI, user testing, prototyping, cross-platform design, marketing
Problem
New customer growth had slowed massively, and ASOS had no referral channel in the US.
My role
Product designer adapting Mention Me's referral journeys to ASOS web, iOS and Android, and testing them with US users.
Key insight
Testing with 18 US participants showed a 10% discount felt too small, and "share by name" confused people. We raised the reward to 15% and focused on link sharing.
Outcome
Launched to US customers as a post-checkout offer on web, iOS and Android.
01

Problem

New customer growth had slowed massively. Referral marketing can bring in loyal, profitable customers, so the challenge was to build a refer-a-friend channel, starting in the US, that rewarded existing customers (referrers) and gave new customers (referees) a reason to make their first purchase.

02

My role

An earlier in-house referral programme had been abused in Russia in 2016, with customers duplicating discount codes. So ASOS partnered with referral specialists Mention Me, who had pre-built journeys. My job was to make those journeys work across ASOS web, iOS and Android, through weekly calls with Mention Me's designers, user flows, imagery, high-fidelity designs and user testing.

03

Process

Mapping the referral journey

The interaction between referrer and referee is complex, so I mapped the mobile web journeys in Overflow, from sharing a link to a referee applying the code at checkout. That covered details like how a shared link appears on Facebook and the pre-filled email text. The flow became the shared reference for product managers and developers.

Imagery that feels like friendship

Imagery was central, especially on desktop. The brand experience team gave me studio shots from Collusion, a unisex label whose models posed together in a relaxed, friendly way. A preference test narrowed down the options: images where the models seemed to look at the offer worked best, drawing the eye to it.

Referrer screens

What an existing customer, the referrer, sees across ASOS.

Referee screens

Testing with US customers

I ran unmoderated tests with 18 US participants, nine on the referrer flow and nine on the referee flow.

Insight

Most participants said a 10% discount was too little, unsurprising in a market shaped by deep discounts like Black Friday, so we raised it to 15% for both sides. "Share by name" also confused people, who thought they could type a friend's name into the promo code box, so link and social sharing became the main route.

  • Participants weren't sure whether to enter their own details or their friend's when signing up, so headings and subheadings were rewritten to make it clear.

04

Outcome

We launched with the offer in the post-checkout experience. I designed native pop-up sliders for iOS and Android and a pop-up animation for web, plus error messages in case Mention Me's API was unavailable. The programme went live to US customers.

Next steps were placing the offer in more areas, such as the ASOS homepage, and giving customers their own referral dashboard in My Account.
